2. Entity Resolution & Disambiguation Protocol

The UK Home Office register publishes corporate names, city/town locations, counties, tier/rating classifications, and visa routes, but omits statutory Companies House Registration Numbers (CRNs).

To join sponsor records with Companies House solvency and insolvency filings without generating catastrophic false positives, we apply a strict multi-tier normalisation and disambiguation pipeline:

Normalisation Rules

  • Legal entity suffixes are normalised to canonical tokens (e.g. LIMITED, LTD, PUBLIC LIMITED COMPANY, PLC, COMMUNITY INTEREST COMPANY, CIC, LLP, LIMITED LIABILITY PARTNERSHIP).
  • Punctuation, diacritics, and redundant whitespace are stripped; corporate articles (The) are evaluated symmetrically.
  • Previous company legal names recorded by Companies House are indexed to resolve entities that rebranded post-licence issuance.

The Disambiguation Principle of Silence: If a normalised sponsor name resolves to multiple active Companies House entities across different registration numbers and geolocation cannot uniquely confirm identity, we display no solvency warning at all. We never guess corporate identity. Showing zero warnings is safer than attributing an insolvency filing to a solvent employer with a common trading name.

3. Solvency Warning Hierarchy

Companies House signals are not generic flags; they reflect specific legal statuses recorded under the Insolvency Act 1986 and Companies Act 2006:

  • Critical Insolvency Warnings (Red): Compulsory liquidation, creditors' voluntary winding up, administration, or active proposal to strike off. These represent urgent risks where the legal employer entity may dissolve.
  • Regulatory Delinquency / Distress (Amber): Statutory accounts overdue or confirmation statements delinquent past the statutory grace period. Often indicates administrative distress or impending strike-off action.
  • Contextual Age Flag: Incorporated within the last 365 days. Not an adverse finding, but relevant for assessing trading history.

4. Ingestion Integrity & Circuit Breakers

Our automated ingestion system runs unattended daily. To prevent corrupted upstream publications, malformed CSV headers, or partial server responses from polluting the ledger, we enforce hard circuit breakers:

  • Volume Floor: If a Home Office download parses fewer than 10,000 valid sponsor records, the pipeline halts immediately, rolls back, and issues an alert.
  • Volatility Ceiling: If the net change in active sponsors exceeds 10% in a single 24-hour cycle, publication is aborted. Real-world daily registry churn is consistently under 0.5%.
  • UTF-8 Byte Repair: Upstream Home Office CSVs periodically contain corrupted single-byte Windows-1252 apostrophes (e.g. \x92). We repair documented encodings while refusing speculative text mutations.

5. Longitudinal Change Tracking

The Home Office publishes a daily snapshot of current holders; it never publishes an official archive of revoked, expired, or surrendered licences. KnowYourSponsor maintains an immutable, daily cryptographic diff archive dating back to August 2026. This allows us to definitively confirm:

  • The exact week an employer was granted a licence.
  • The exact week a licence ceased to appear on the official register.
  • Rating downgrades from A-rating (Full Compliance) to B-rating (Home Office Action Plan).

6. Boundaries of Public Register Data

What KnowYourSponsor Cannot Determine:

  • Hiring Intent: Holding a sponsor licence proves legal authority to issue Certificates of Sponsorship (CoS); it does not indicate whether active job vacancies exist.
  • Licence Revocation Reason: The Home Office does not state whether an employer left the register due to voluntary surrender, non-renewal, company restructuring, or punitive revocation.
  • Individual Eligibility: Sponsoring capability depends on individual candidate salary, SOC code threshold alignment, and visa route compliance.
